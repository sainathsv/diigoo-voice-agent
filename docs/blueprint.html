JENAI Platform Blueprint

Version 1 for founder approval · 19 Sep 2026 · Internal

What this is. The plan for turning JENAI from a thin panel around a voice engine into a proper multi-tenant platform: an ERP for Indian businesses whose core function is AI calling, with full teams on both sides (Diigoo's own staff and every client's staff).

How it was made. Twelve research tracks ran in parallel: organisation and roles, the competitor market, telephony and integrations, target architecture, how large SaaS companies run reliability, engineering process, security and compliance, AI agent quality, the customer lifecycle, scale and unit economics, plus two audits of what JENAI runs today (the code, and the live servers). Each claim below comes from those reports; items that could not be verified are marked as such.

An honest limit. No SaaS, including the largest, runs with "no problems at all". What they do, and what this plan does, is catch problems before release, keep any failure contained to one client, and recover quickly and visibly. That is the standard used throughout.

Part 2. What JENAI is

An AI-calling ERP: JENAI owns the thin system of record a front office runs on (calls, contacts, calendar, inbox, campaigns, payments) and connects to the heavy systems clients already have (Practo, Eka Care, Petpooja, hospital software) instead of rebuilding them.

This is the pattern proven by Weave, Podium, NexHealth, Zenoti and GoHighLevel: the phone and the AI sit in front, the business records sit behind, and every team works in one place. The Indian voice-AI vendors (Bolna, Ringg, Smallest, Sarvam) mostly stop at the agent and a dashboard, which is the gap JENAI fills.

Part 3. Teams, roles and access

Two ideas from Salesforce, Freshdesk and Zenoti carry the design: role decides what a person can do, scope decides which records they see (whole organisation, certain branches, or only their own). A person's rights are the union of their roles. Clients get these templates and can clone and edit them in a module-by-level grid (NexHealth's pattern).

How Diigoo staff reach client data

  1. No standing access. Staff see settings, usage and masked call metadata by role. Personal data needs a grant.
  2. Read-only support session. Ticket number, reason and client consent (clients can pre-allow "JENAI support for N days", like Salesforce). 30 to 60 minutes; writes are refused on the server.
  3. Write session. Client consent plus a second approver; 30 minutes; never touches owners, billing, API keys, numbers, exports or deletes.
  4. Break-glass. Super admin only, for live incidents or legal holds; the client owner is alerted at once; reviewed within 48 hours.
  5. Audit. Every action records both the staff member and the impersonated user (GoHighLevel logs only the latter, a known gap). Clients see their own support-access log; logs kept at least a year.

Login system: Better Auth (MIT, runs in JENAI's own Postgres, India-resident) with its organisation, admin, SSO, SCIM and passkey plugins. Auth.js is now in maintenance mode under the same team. Clerk limits custom roles per app, not per client; WorkOS and Keycloak were the alternatives considered.

Part 5. Numbers, integrations and compliance

New rule, 18 Sep 2026. TRAI's third amendment to the telemarketing regulations treats any call placed by software, including AI voice, as an "A2P call". The business must declare A2P use and its caller-ID numbers to the telecom operator in advance; undeclared calls count as spam. A2P calls outside the 140 and 1600 series can carry a termination charge of up to ₹0.05 per minute, and operators must run AI/ML spam detection. The effective date was not in the coverage found. JENAI's live outbound numbers should be declared with Vobiz now.

Three ways a client's phone number connects

ModeWho it fitsHow it works
Managed (default)Clinics, spas, gyms, restaurantsOne Vobiz sub-account per client, KYC'd in the client's own name through Vobiz's hosted KYC and Partner API. Calling stays blocked until KYC completes. Replaces today's single shared account.
Client's own providerHospitals, GHMC, anyone already on Exotel, Tata or OzonetelJENAI stores their API credentials in the vault and points their call flow or Voicebot applet at a JENAI WebSocket.
Forwarding or own carrierBusinesses keeping their public numberThe existing number forwards (busy, no answer or always) to the JENAI number; SIP through Vobiz only where the client has an IP-PBX.

Twilio cannot carry domestic India calls. Telnyx launched India numbers on 17 Sep 2026 in four cities only: watch, don't depend. Provider shortlist: Vobiz primary, Exotel for bring-your-own and as second carrier, Plivo for redundancy, Tata Smartflo for hospitals and government.

Onboarding a client's number (managed mode)

  1. Create the client's Vobiz sub-account by API; credentials go into the vault; calling blocked.
  2. Send the hosted KYC link (PAN, GST, CIN or DigiLocker); collect the data processing agreement and a use declaration: inbound only, outbound service, or outbound promotional.
  3. Choose the number: local landline for inbound and service, 140-series for promotional (needs DLT entity), 1600 for financial or government clients, or forwarding from the existing number.
  4. File the A2P declaration per caller ID with a timestamped record; for promotional use, add DLT headers and templates and link JENAI as telemarketer.
  5. Create a Vobiz application inside the client's sub-account with a signed, client-specific answer URL.
  6. Map number to agent; set disclosure, recording, hours, transfer number and concurrency limits.
  7. Automatic test calls in and out, confirm transcript and CRM write-back, top up the wallet, go live.

The gate every outbound call passes

Before any dial: campaign active; inside the branch's calling window and the platform's promotional window; not on DND/NCPR or the client's opt-out list; valid consent for this purpose (enquiry consent lasts 7 days); frequency caps per contact; caller ID declared for A2P and the right series for the purpose; wallet balance reserved. A spoken "don't call me" is detected and suppressed immediately across the client.

Dialer design

Progressive dialing only: a voice-worker slot is reserved before the number is dialled, so every answered call has an agent and there are no silent calls (silent calls drive spam complaints, and under the new rules a few complaints can trigger action on a number). Each tick picks the next tenant by weighted round robin, runs the compliance gate, reserves wallet balance, then atomically acquires all capacity leases (global model sessions, carrier channels, per-number concurrency and calls-per-second, tenant and campaign limits, a per-phone lock, and 20% held back for inbound). Inbound always wins; when inbound surges, outbound backs off.

Part 7. Reliability standard

The pattern at Salesforce, Atlassian, Slack, Shopify and Stripe is the same: a tenant ID on every row and request, multi-AZ by default, gradual rollouts of both code and config, restores that are actually tested, per-tenant limits, and SLOs with a formal incident process. Cells, sharding and multi-region come later, when scale demands them. Notion ran on one Postgres for five years before sharding.

When a month's error budget is spent, releases freeze except for critical and security fixes.

Incidents

SEV1: no client (or GHMC) can make or receive calls, a breach, or data loss. SEV2: a major feature down for many clients or one enterprise client fully down. SEV3: partial degradation. SEV1 is acknowledged within 15 minutes, the status page updated within 30, and every SEV1 and SEV2 gets a blameless review within five working days. India adds a hard clock: cyber incidents go to CERT-In within 6 hours.

Deploying without dropping live calls

  1. Voice workers run as their own fleet, each advertising its free call slots.
  2. A deploy marks a worker "not ready" (no new calls), waits for active calls to finish up to the maximum call length, then exits.
  3. New versions take 1 to 5% of new calls first; call setup, latency and drop rate are compared before going further.
  4. Under load, shed in this order: outbound campaigns, then analytics and learning jobs. Never inbound.
  5. The carrier's fallback URL points at a degraded mode (message plus callback queue), so a caller never hears dead air.

Disaster recovery

Stage 1: database failover in 60 to 120 seconds with point-in-time recovery (RPO 5 min); region loss restored from Hyderabad backups within 24 h. Stage 2: a pilot-light copy in Hyderabad (RPO 15 min, RTO 4 h). Restore drills monthly; a full game day twice a year. Atlassian's April 2022 outage (775 customers, up to 14 days) is the lesson: soft deletes and a tested single-tenant restore.

Part 8. Engineering standard

DORA's 2025 research found AI coding amplifies whatever process a team already has: faster delivery, but more instability without discipline. Stripe merges over 1,000 agent-written changes a week, each reviewed by a human, with agents on isolated machines that never see production. That is the model.

AreaStandard
RepositoriesOn git.diigoo.com: jenai/platform (monorepo: apps, packages, infra, docs, ADRs, runbooks) and jenai/voice-engine (the Dograh fork). The panel and Vaani are archived read-only once replaced. Never two live codebases.
Upstream forkA pure upstream mirror branch plus jenai/main built from an upstream release and local patches, each tagged UPSTREAM: <carry>, <PR#> or <drop> (OpenShift's method), with a PATCHES.md ledger. Rebased per upstream release, gated by voice evals.
BranchingTrunk-based: main always deployable, branches live under 2 days, merge requests of about 200 reviewed lines.
ReviewGitLab's free edition cannot enforce approvals, so: nobody pushes to main, only named maintainers merge, pipelines and all threads must pass. An AI review runs first in a fresh context; a human merges. Risky paths (migrations, auth, tenant isolation, billing, telephony, infra, agent config) need a second human or a 24-hour cool-off.
CI stagesVerify under 10 min (types, lint, unit tests, secret scan, dependency audit, migration lint, API breaking-change check); integration tests that prove tenant A cannot read tenant B; images tagged by commit; a preview app per merge request; nightly load and security scans.
EnvironmentsLocal, preview per change, staging (own database, keys and test number), production. No more rsync: production pulls signed images through CI only, with a one-command rollback.
Database changesGenerated migrations only; expand and contract across two deploys; indexes built concurrently; backfills as jobs; a point-in-time backup before each migration.

No production credentials or real customer data anywhere an agent runs. Tests in the same change; bug fixes start with a failing test. At most two CI rounds per agent run, then a human takes over.

Part 9. AI agent quality

Sierra, Decagon, ElevenLabs, Retell and PolyAI all use one release model: every version is an immutable snapshot, a named pointer decides which version is live, simulated-caller tests gate each release, a small share of traffic gets it first, and rollback moves the pointer back. JENAI's past incidents map directly onto it.

Part 12. Scale and unit economics

At 100,000 calls a day, the cost is carriers, speech and models, not servers: worker compute is under 2% of cost per minute. The binding limits are vendor concurrency caps, which need enterprise contracts before the 50k-a-day stage.

The biggest unknown is today's real cost. Gemini Live's cost per minute is uncertain by about 4x, because long sessions may rebill context every turn. One week of logging usage per call on current traffic settles it. Until then no price should be published.

Recommendation: keep Gemini Live for launch (proven in Telugu, lowest latency) with context compression, capped call length and per-call cost metering; build the hosted pipeline now as an automatic fallback and a cheaper option per client; move high-volume clients to hybrid or self-hosted only when open Telugu TTS passes quality tests. Replace burstable t3 servers with steady c7i/c7a instances: burstable machines have been seen throttling calls into silence.

Part 13. The build plan

A strangler migration: the new platform grows beside the live one and takes over one client and one number at a time. Outbound cutovers happen at night when promotional dialing is not allowed anyway; inbound moves one number at a time with instant rollback; every step is behind a per-client switch. Durations are estimates for a small team working with AI coding agents.

Phase 0Stabilise · 1 to 2 weeks
  • Done Backups, protection, alerts. Tonight Certificate renewal fix.
  • Put all JENAI code in git on git.diigoo.com (needs the pending server launch); staging copy of production.
  • Publish on every agent edit so inbound gets changes; persist the dial queue; hash passwords; remove the password-only login.
  • Lock down recording storage; stop sending never-expiring recording links to the browser.
  • Containers restart on their own after a reboot; free disk space; find the CPU climb since 4 Sep.
  • Move the helper model off the GPU box, then stop it (saves about ₹79k a month).
